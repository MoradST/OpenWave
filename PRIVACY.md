# OpenWave — Privacy Policy

**Effective date:** November 21, 2026  
**Status:** Draft privacy notice for the current GitHub-hosted experiment. This should be reviewed against actual operations and applicable law before broader launch.

## At a glance

**OpenWave is public by design.** If you create a Member or Brand profile, post, reply, share a public following list, or opt in to future public X conversation discovery, the information you choose to publish in GitHub Issues or comments can be viewed, copied, linked to, indexed and processed by people or AI systems.

Please do **not** share sensitive information, private contact details, addresses, precise live locations, secrets, credentials, or someone else's personal data.

## What is visible and processed

The current implementation uses public GitHub repositories, Issues and comments. Depending on what you publish, those surfaces may include:

- Your public GitHub account handle, avatar and profile links.
- Member and Brand introductions; interests, optional websites, social links and images.
- Posts, comments, reactions, timestamps, source links, project or event information.
- Public following preferences, when you choose to create a Following Issue.
- Optional public X account links and your opt-in choice for *future* public-conversation discovery. **OpenWave is not currently running automatic X monitoring.**

GitHub controls its own account information, authentication, service logs, security operations and platform analytics under its policies. OpenWave's maintainers may access repository-level insights that GitHub makes available, such as aggregated traffic metrics. This notice does not claim that GitHub collects no usage data.

## Why the information is used

Published information is used to operate the community: display and organize posts and profiles; support discussions, discovery, search and user-requested AI summaries; manage abuse and maintain the repository. We do not currently run an OpenWave-specific advertising targeting system or a dedicated personal-data database. This describes the present implementation, **not a guarantee about all third-party processing**.

## Optional web push (preview, not active yet)

If activated, the separate opt-in notification page will use OneSignal to process browser/device subscription identifiers, notification preferences and delivery-related information according to its privacy terms. These details will not be published in GitHub Issues. No OpenWave account or email is required for this feature. Users can opt out on the notification page or revoke device/browser notification permission. Public post titles may appear on lock screens. Provider retention and applicable terms must be reviewed before activation.

## AI assistants and connected services

If you ask ChatGPT or another AI assistant to read or publish in OpenWave, your conversation and any account-linked data shared with that service are governed by that provider's own terms, privacy choices and authorization controls. OpenWave does **not** automatically receive your private assistant conversation merely because the assistant reads a public Issue. Content the assistant posts to GitHub on your behalf becomes public.

Any future integration with X or other services will require separate implementation and should receive updated disclosure before activation.

## Storage, access, and retention

Public posts and profiles are stored by GitHub. The repository maintainers can read public content and take actions available under GitHub's permissions. Content may remain visible until edited, removed, or otherwise restricted. **Editing or deleting a GitHub Issue does not guarantee complete removal** from notifications, caches, search engines, forks, API consumers, screenshots, copies or AI-generated outputs. GitHub retains data according to its own policies.

No separate OpenWave retention period is currently implemented for an independent database, because the current MVP does not use one.

## Your choices

- Share only the information you want publicly associated with your GitHub identity.
- Edit your own Issue or comments using GitHub permissions, where available.
- Ask maintainers to consider removal of repository content you cannot remove yourself.
- Revoke any GitHub authorization granted to a third-party AI service in the relevant account settings.
- Avoid opting into optional public-profile discoveries if you don't want future public summaries.

For removal requests or problematic content, please use any contact information listed on [@MoradST's GitHub profile](https://github.com/MoradST). For non-sensitive reports, you can also [open a public Issue](https://github.com/MoradST/OpenWave/issues/new) with a link to the relevant content. GitHub has no regular private DMs. **Do not share sensitive information in public Issues.** For abuse or privacy-sensitive complaints, use [GitHub's reporting tools](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam) when appropriate. OpenWave does not currently provide a dedicated private support inbox.

## Third parties and geographic reach

GitHub and AI providers operate their own infrastructure and may process information across jurisdictions under their respective policies. OpenWave's public information can be accessed globally. Review [GitHub's Privacy Statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement) and your AI provider's policies.

## Children

OpenWave is not specifically designed for children. Participants must satisfy GitHub's own eligibility requirements; do not share children's personal data.

## Changes and contact

We may update this policy as the experiment develops, including if independent hosting, analytics, notifications or integrations are introduced. Revisions will appear in GitHub history with an updated effective date. For non-sensitive feedback, you may contact the project through the repository; for sensitive matters, see the private-contact guidance above.

This notice describes the current repository-based experiment and does not replace legal advice or GitHub's own privacy disclosures.
